@section(proto)
Interference is the mechanism by which two waves meeting in the same medium reinforce or cancel depending on their phase relationship.
In @term(music) this produces consonance and dissonance, the reason certain intervals feel stable and others tense is that their interference patterns are periodic and regular, or not.
@image(030-interference,The double-slit interference pattern,Wikimedia Commons) In the @term(double slit, double-slit, "Thomas Young's 1801 experiment in which light passing through two narrow slits produces an interference pattern of bands on a screen behind them. The pattern can only be explained if light spreads as a wave from each slit and the waves combine. Later extended to electrons, atoms, and even small molecules, showing that wave behavior is universal at the quantum scale.") experiment it produces the banded pattern on the screen that proves light is a wave phenomenon. In the brain, millions of neurons continuously disturbing the electromagnetic @term(field, fields, "the unified Maxwell field that pervades all space, of which electricity, magnetism, and light are aspects. The medium through which charged particles, including neurons, interact and through which their disturbances propagate as overlapping waves.") produce overlapping interference patterns whose structure may be what we call thought.

The same mathematics describes all three cases.
The question is whether this is analogy or identity, whether @term(harmony, harmony, "the experience of elements fitting together with a sense of inevitability, governed by simple ratios between their frequencies. In music: the overlapping wave patterns of consonant intervals. In thought: possibly the same wave-overlap structure expressed in neural firing rather than air pressure.") and diffraction, and cognition are genuinely the same phenomenon at different scales, or merely share a formal description.
That question is left open here.
But the formal identity is already surprising enough: the ear detecting consonance and the screen detecting the double slit pattern are both reading the same kind of structure from the same kind of process.

Note: the double slit sits between two limiting cases.
A rock thrown in a lake produces a single disturbance, impulse, ripples, silence.
A boiling lake produces continuous thermal agitation from everywhere at once.
The double slit is a controlled, repeating source: two points, interfering continuously, producing a stable pattern.
The brain is closer to the boiling lake, but the pattern that emerges may be no less structured for that.
